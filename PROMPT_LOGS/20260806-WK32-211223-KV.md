### 21:12:50 - Response

Interesting signal. On a 4-core system, load 0.3-0.66 means the machine is almost entirely idle from a scheduling perspective — most of the runnable time is spent in `S` (sleeping, waiting on I/O), not `R` (actively running).

Combined with your htop observation (all 4 cores CYCLING between 0-100%): pgloader is **network-latency-bound**, not compute-bound. Each worker sends a batch to RDS, blocks waiting for the WAL flush + ACK round-trip, then processes the next batch. Only briefly on-CPU each cycle. That's why htop shows peaks but load stays low.

**Meaningful conclusion:** the migrate-host is NOT the bottleneck. RDS commit-and-ACK latency is the pacing factor. Bumping concurrency higher (say 8 workers) would just mean more workers waiting in parallel — not faster throughput.

**If we ever want to squeeze more speed:** the next tuning frontier is RDS-side, not migrate-host — larger RDS instance class (more IOPS + faster commit), or temporarily setting `synchronous_commit=off` on the RDS side for the duration of the migration. But `concurrency=4` + `batch=5000` at this network latency is already near the sweet spot.

For today's timing test: expect the wall-clock to land near the middle of the 8-12 min design target, not the bottom. RDS is the throttle.
