### 21:09:46 - Response

That's textbook. 5.5 GB used on 32 GB = 17% — massive headroom. The picture:

- MariaDB's 8 GB `innodb_buffer_pool_size` is a max cap, not a static allocation. It grows RSS on-demand as InnoDB reads hot pages, currently probably sitting around 2-3 GB of the 8 GB ceiling.
- pgloader's SBCL heap is `--dynamic-space-size 10240` (10 GB) but committed memory grows only as needed — likely 2-3 GB active for the 4 concurrent worker sets.
- Plus OS + everything else ~500 MB.

Zero swap pressure. Design ceilings were sized for worst-case (bigger tables in the future, or a client DB much larger than yours). The comfortable margin means we could push `concurrency=6` or `=8` in a future tuning pass if we ever want to squeeze more parallelism — but 4 was the safe design-doc number and it's working. Save those knobs for if you ever hit a 50M-row target.
