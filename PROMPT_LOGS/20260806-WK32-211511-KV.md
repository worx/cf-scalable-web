### 21:15:47 - Response

Tempting, and your observation is real — 5.5 GB mid-run is nowhere near the ceiling. But I'd hold off, for three reasons:

**1. That 5.5 GB is mid-load, not peak.** The pgloader phase you're watching now is bulk data ingest. There's a second, memorable spike coming: **`create indexes` and `foreign keys` fire AFTER all rows land**. SBCL sorts intermediate keys into memory during index builds. Historically that's where pgloader's memory grows the most on any run. Come back with peak-during-index-phase numbers before we resize.

**2. Downsizing to 16 GB forces tuning cuts that undo Phase 2's wins.** The current numbers:
   - MariaDB `innodb_buffer_pool_size = 8G`  
   - pgloader `--dynamic-space-size 10240` (10 GB)  
   - Total ceiling: 18+ GB  
   - Headroom on 32 GB: comfortable  
   - Headroom on 16 GB: **negative** — you'd have to drop the buffer pool to 4-5G and the heap to 6-7G, which walks back the biggest speed wins we just measured.

**3. The math doesn't move the needle.** `r7g.xlarge → m7g.xlarge` (same CPU, half memory) saves ~$0.05/hr. Ephemeral migrate-host runs ~1 hour per invocation. That's **$0.05 per migration saved**. An OOM during migration costs ~90 min of your time to redo. Not worth the risk-reward.

**Better save-money angle if you want it:** Phase 5 shrinks the deploy-host from `t4g.small` → `t4g.micro` because MariaDB + pgloader will no longer live there. That's a *continuous* saving (deploy-host runs 24/7) vs migrate-host's ~10x-per-month. Way more impactful.

Recommendation: keep r7g.xlarge for now. Watch peak during index-build phase of THIS run. If peak stays under ~10 GB across a few runs including larger client DBs, revisit m7g.xlarge later. If it ever crosses 12 GB, we're glad we stayed on r7g.
