Interesting flip. During row loads, workers were network-blocked → load 0.3-0.6. Now during index builds, workers are compute-blocked → load 3+ on a 4-core box. **This is where concurrency=4 actually pays off** — four index sorts running in parallel on four cores. Without concurrency, indexes would build serially and this phase would take 4x longer.

So the wall time is composed of two very different phases:
- **Phase A (row loads):** RDS commit-bound. Compute idle. Slow relative to what migrate-host could do.
- **Phase B (index builds):** Compute-bound. All cores pinned. Fast relative to serial baseline.

Phase 2's concurrency=4 tuning is delivering real value here, not during row loads. That reframes the earlier "Phase 2 didn't help pgloader" conclusion — it helped a lot, just in a different phase than expected.

You're likely minutes from done. Sequences + FKs are quick after indexes complete.
