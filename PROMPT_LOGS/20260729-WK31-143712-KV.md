## Three ways to make it self-healing (in decreasing order of "industry-standard")

1. **Lambda-backed CFN custom resource** that empties versioned objects on stack delete. About 30-50 lines of inline Python + IAM policy in the YAML. This is the canonical AWS pattern for "delete a bucket that will always have objects at destroy time." Not southern engineering — it's the recognized answer.

2. **`LifecycleConfiguration` with `NoncurrentVersionExpiration: 1` day.** Native S3 feature. Set-and-forget. Trade-off: a destroy-all fired within 24 hours of a sync would still hit the current problem, so this alone isn't enough.

3. **Disable versioning entirely** (via a per-env parameter, on for prod, off for sandbox/staging). Simplest change. Trade-off: loses accidental-overwrite protection for whatever env you turn it off in — probably fine for sandbox since prod is source of truth.

**My recommendation:** #1 (Lambda custom resource) — proper long-term architectural answer. Add #2 as belt-and-suspenders for prod (keeps version storage in check even without a destroy).
